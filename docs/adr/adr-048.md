# ADR-048 — Multi-household-ready contracts while staying family-first

> **Status:** Accepted
> **Baseline:** October 8, 2026
> **Origin:** Owner decision during the October 8 planning review: family-first now, contracts ready for multiple households later; revised after an independent final review
> **Legacy references:** None
> **Supersedes / superseded by:** None. Builds on [ADR-007](adr-007.md), [ADR-031](adr-031.md) and [ADR-034](adr-034.md)

## Context

Nilumi serves one family. The owner wants a path to other households without
rewriting it later. The [October 8 review](../research/04-research.md#platform-constraints)
notes three points:
- DPDP §3(c)(i) excludes processing for personal or domestic purposes.
- Most obligations under the Rules are reported to start around 13 May 2027. This comes from a secondary source.
- Processing another household's data plausibly falls outside that exclusion.

None of the reference repositories isolates members inside one household.

## Decision

**Isolation.** Keep `household_id` on every household-owned row, enforced by
RLS ([ADR-007](adr-007.md)). The household always comes from the session,
never from the client. No query crosses households. Decided before the pilot
(owner request, October 8):
- Every household-owned table has `household_id not null`, including child, ledger and join tables. Only `households` and the global registries (`predicates`, `embedding_configs`, `run_state_transitions`) have none.
- Every reference between household-owned rows is a composite foreign key, `(household_id, parent_id)`, against a parent's `unique (household_id, id)`.
- Member-ID arrays and polymorphic references, which cannot carry foreign keys, are checked by triggers against the same household.
- A CI schema test enforces all three ([Architecture §9](../core/02-architecture.md#9-data-model)).

**Roles.** Keep generic role names:
- `admin` as a capability (`members.is_admin`, adults only), not a member kind. The founding adult gets it at household creation; only an existing admin grants or removes it, never to themselves, and the last admin cannot be removed. It never widens row visibility.
- member kinds `adult` and `child`
- `helper` later, under [ADR-034](adr-034.md)

Family relationships such as "my wife" stay as data used for speaker-relative
resolution, not as role names in the schema.

**Reserved contracts.** Reserve the following as schema designs and
documentation only. They are not created before the pilot:
- `resource_grants`
- `delegations`
- `consents`

The consent lifecycle records the grantor, grantee, scope, purpose, expiry,
revocation and an audit trail.

**Visibility.** Keep [ADR-031](adr-031.md)'s defaults:
- household visibility for preferences and family facts
- "for me" items private
- private items do not reveal that they exist

Explicit sharing stays under [ADR-024](adr-024.md).

**Legal.** Rely on the domestic exemption while only the owner's family uses
Nilumi. **Before onboarding any external household, whatever the date,**
obtain primary legal verification of DPDP applicability and obligations.

## Alternatives

- **Build multi-tenant features now.** Rejected: there is no second household, and it would delay the pilot.
- **Stay strictly single-household.** Rejected: it would make the later move costly.
- **Make everything private by default and share by grant.** Rejected: it contradicts ADR-031 and adds friction to ordinary family facts.

## Consequences

Schemas and tests already assume more than one household, so RLS tests include
a second household. The reserved contracts carry no runtime cost. External
onboarding stays blocked on a legal gate in the roadmap, and on the
[ADR-046](adr-046.md) production privacy gate (ZDR on every LLM and embedding
request).
